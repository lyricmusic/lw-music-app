# Product audit

Use this mode to find business and product opportunities in an existing application. The output is a prioritized opportunity map, not an unbounded feature wishlist.

## Establish the audit frame

Confirm the product objective, target users, business model, planning horizon, and decision the audit should support. Inventory available evidence and explicitly list important missing data.

Trace the actual product journeys from repository evidence and supplied artifacts. Evaluate only flows that can be verified; mark inferred user behavior as an assumption.

## Evaluate the product

Review the relevant parts of this system:

1. Value proposition and target segments: who gets value, which problem is solved, and why this product is preferable to the current alternative.
2. Acquisition and entry: how users discover, understand, join, or start using the product.
3. Activation and time to value: the first meaningful outcome, friction before it, and whether the product makes the next action clear.
4. Core loop and retention: the behavior users repeat, reasons to return, failure states, and lifecycle gaps.
5. Collaboration, sharing, and referral: where multi-user value or organic distribution exists and where it breaks.
6. Monetization and cost-to-serve: current or plausible value capture, usage costs, abuse exposure, and operational burden. Do not assume that monetization is required when the business objective says otherwise.
7. Trust, safety, moderation, privacy, and control: product risks that can damage adoption, retention, or reputation.
8. Measurement readiness: whether important behaviors have definitions, baselines, event coverage, cohorts, and guardrails.

## Record findings

For each meaningful finding, state:

- affected user or segment;
- journey stage and verified current behavior;
- friction or unmet job;
- evidence and confidence;
- expected user and business impact;
- recommended opportunity, not a preselected implementation;
- smallest way to validate it;
- dependencies, risks, and guardrails.

Group related findings into opportunity themes. Rank no more than the few opportunities that deserve near-term attention. Explain why lower-ranked items should wait.

## Audit output

Return:

1. Executive diagnosis.
2. Evidence inventory and gaps.
3. Verified journey and funnel observations.
4. Prioritized opportunity map.
5. Recommended discovery or measurement work.
6. Candidate feature decisions that should proceed to `feature-decision.md`.

Do not claim that a repository-only audit proves demand, retention impact, willingness to pay, or market size.
